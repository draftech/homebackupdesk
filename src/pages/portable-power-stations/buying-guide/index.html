Buying guide · Power stations

Buy surge headroom first, watt-hours second, brand third.

Portable power stations are simple products sold with complicated numbers. This page is the order of operations we wish every product page used.

  1. Running watts. Add the loads that will be on together. A fridge might be 150–250 W once spinning; a microwave is a short 1,000 W punch.
  2. Starting watts. Compressors and pumps can demand several times running watts for a second. If the inverter cannot do that second, the fridge never starts and the “2,000 Wh” never matters.
  3. Watt-hours. Rough runtime hours ≈ usable Wh ÷ running watts. Usable is not always the nameplate. LiFePO4 often lets you plan closer to 80–90% than old NMC packing.
  4. Recharge. Wall watts decide how fast you recover between flickers. Solar watts are weather. Open-circuit voltage (Voc) of the panel must stay under the station’s solar input maximum — this is the expensive mistake.
  5. Expandability. Extra batteries beat guessing a 6 kWh outage on day one if the vendor’s extra pack is real and in stock.
  6. Ports. UPS pass-through, 12 V for fridges/CPAPs, and whether you need a 30 A TT-30 are lifestyle details, not afterthoughts.

Who this guide is for

  • First-time buyers in the Jackery / Bluetti / Anker SOLIX aisle.
  • People replacing a loud jobsite generator for indoor-adjacent essentials.
