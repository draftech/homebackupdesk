Silo 1 · Whole-house generators

Standby generators are a construction project with a motor attached.

A whole-house generator is the right tool when the house itself has to stay alive: furnace or air handler, well pump, sump, freezer, and maybe one HVAC condenser. It is the wrong tool when you only need a fridge and a router.

This silo exists so the topical cluster on The Home Backup is honest. Plenty of search traffic wants “best whole house generator 2026” and a buy button. Who this silo is for

  • Homes with 240 V loads that a 120 V power station cannot feed — wells, some air handlers, dryers you refuse to lose.
  • Multi-day winter outages where propane or natural gas still flows after the electric grid dies.
  • Medical, accessibility, or sump loads that cannot wait on a person to roll a machine into the rain at 2 a.m.
  • Owners who will treat the unit as a plant: exercise, oil, and a service call, not a rusted lawn ornament.

Who should skip it

  • Renters, tight lots, or HOAs that will fight an outdoor plant before you pour a pad.
  • Shoppers whose real budget is $1–3k all-in. That is a power station job, not a transfer switch.
  • Anyone who actually wanted a roof-tied wall pack. Tesla Powerwall is out of scope on this desk.
  • People who will not maintain an engine. An unexercised standby is a very expensive paperweight.

What “whole house” actually means

Dealers use “whole house” as a comfort phrase. Electricians mean: an automatic transfer switch (ATS) that moves the service or a managed panel onto a generator, plus an engine sized for the loads you refuse to shed. A 10 kW air-cooled unit can be “whole house” if the condenser is locked out. A 22 kW liquid-cooled unit can still brown out a heat pump if locked-rotor amps were ignored. Nameplate kilowatts are a starting class, not a promise that the outdoor unit will start in January.

Fuel is part of the machine. Natural gas is set-and-forget when the utility stays up — which it often does in wind events. Propane is independent of the gas company and finite in the tank. Gasoline belongs on portables, not on a pad you expect to start itself. Placement, noise, and the AHJ (authority having jurisdiction) kill otherwise “best” quotes.
