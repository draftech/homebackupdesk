Method

We rank for the job, not for the press photo.

A 2 kWh box that cannot start a fridge is a paperweight. This page is the rubric on every money page.

What “review” means on a new site

We do not claim a warehouse full of long-term teardowns we did not do. We combine manufacturer electrical ratings, documented buyer failure modes (surge, open-circuit voltage, transfer-switch wiring), and category constraints. When we have not run a unit for a season, we say so.

Ranking factors

  1. The job. Fridge + network, medical devices, 240 V well pump, or whole-home HVAC are different products.
  2. Continuous watts vs surge. Compressors and well pumps care about the first second.
  3. Usable watt-hours. Nameplate capacity is not the same as a 90% discharge on LiFePO4.
  4. Expandability and 120/240 V. A station that stays 120 V cannot feed a split-phase dryer no matter the marketing.
  5. Recharge paths. Wall AC, alternator, and portable panels with a Voc the station will actually accept.
  6. Support and warranty. Four-figure bricks need parts and firmware, not just a glossy app.
  7. Program quality. We feature 30-day-cookie brands (Jackery, Bluetti, Anker SOLIX). We do not feature EcoFlow while the cookie is a 7-day risk.

Caveats we will not sand off

Portable stations are not silent whole-home plants. Standby generators need permits, pads, and oil changes. “Home batteries” on this site are mostly expandable power-station ecosystems — not Tesla Powerwall. Rooftop solar is out of scope.

Who a page is for

Every money page includes a who-it’s-for / who-should-skip pair. If a $4,000 station is the wrong tool, we say so and point at whole-house generators or a smaller power station.
