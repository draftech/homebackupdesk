The job this class actually does

A portable power station is a battery, an inverter, and a set of outlets. People call them solar generators. The sun is optional. The inverter is not.

The job on this page is narrow on purpose. Keep a refrigerator cycling. Keep an ONT and Wi-Fi up. Keep phones, a laptop, LED lamps, and a CPAP from dying in the first night. That is a 120 V essentials list, not a house. Central air, a dryer, and a 240 V well are different products. We will send those shoppers away instead of stretching a 2 kWh brick.

Three current SKUs sit in that class on thehomebackup.com. They are close on watt-hours. They are not close on weight, continuous watts, extra-battery maps, or whether the manufacturer even published a surge number we could copy. Rankings here are job assignments, not a press-photo score. Method: how we review.

Who the Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus is for

Buy the Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus when the fridge-and-essentials job is the point and you want the highest continuous inverter in this trio. Jackery lists 2,042.8 Wh and 3,000 W continuous. Voltage is 120 V. Chemistry is LiFePO4. The case is 61.5 lb. Expansion is the Battery Pack 2000 Plus, not a 240 V trick. Street price on the manufacturer page was $899, with $2,199 shown as the compare-at figure, on August 26, 2026.

That extra 600 W of continuous headroom versus the 2,400 W pair is the practical reason to pay attention. A microwave and a fridge should not share a thin inverter. 3,000 W does not make this a well-pump machine. It does make simultaneous 120 V loads less of a gamble, as long as you still test the compressor start in your kitchen.

Surge is blank in our table. Jackery’s US page did not give us one number we could copy without picking a side in a conflict. If a listing elsewhere shouts a peak watt, ignore it until Jackery prints one figure on the product page. We would rather leave the cell empty than invent marketing.

Stay on Jackery if you also want the in-family folding panel. Pairing notes live in the review and in the portable panel best-of. The SolarSaga 200W is the named folder we carry for this brand. Do not series-wire a random blanket into the input because a marketplace title said 200 W.

Who the BLUETTI AC200L is for

Buy the BLUETTI AC200L when the same ~2 kWh job is the starting point and the outage story is “add watt-hours later.” BLUETTI lists 2,048 Wh and 2,400 W. Voltage is 120 V. Chemistry is LiFePO4. Weight is 62.4 lb. The expansion ceiling on the manufacturer page is 8,192 Wh. Street price was $899 on August 26, 2026. No compare-at figure was on that page, so we do not invent one.

8,192 Wh is the reason this SKU shows up next to home-backup conversations. It is still 120 V. Extra modules do not turn the inverter into split-phase. If your well, dryer, or air handler is 240 V, this is the wrong aisle even at 8 kWh. If your problem is a long 120 V outage — fridge, freezer, network, lights — modular capacity is the honest lever.

Surge is blank here too. The live BLUETTI page was unclear or conflicting, so we left it empty. Test the fridge. Do not trust a roundup that filled in a peak watt we would not print.

Stay on BLUETTI if you will actually buy extra batteries and a matched panel. The named folder is the SP200L. Voc on that panel is 24.6 V; still check the station’s solar window before you series anything. Matching rules: portable panel buying guide.

Who the Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 is for

Buy the Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 when you want the same 2,048 Wh class in a lighter case, and you will not pretend 120 V is 240 V. Anker lists 2,400 W continuous and 4,000 W peak. Chemistry is LFP — the same lithium iron phosphate family as LiFePO4. Weight is 41.7 lb. Expansion is up to 4 kWh. Street price was $999.99 on August 26, 2026.

41.7 lb is the differentiator you can feel on a staircase. The Jackery is 61.5 lb. The BLUETTI is 62.4 lb. If the station lives on a garage floor, that gap is trivia. If you will carry it to a second-floor kitchen or into a car, it is the whole review.

Peak is the other honest number in this trio. Anker printed 4,000 W peak. We copy it. That is still not a license to skip a fridge test. Peak is a second, not a plan for a well.

Expansion stops at 4 kWh in this family. That is less ceiling than the AC200L’s 8,192 Wh. It is enough for a longer 120 V outage than the base pack alone. It is not a reason to skip the Anker SOLIX F3800 if you actually need 120/240 V.

The named Anker folder is the PS400. It is 400 W nameplate and 35.3 lb. Confirm the station’s solar input before you treat that as a default. Panel silo: portable solar panels.

Who the 2 kWh class is for

  • You have a written 120 V list: fridge, network, lights, maybe CPAP.
  • You will test the compressor start once, on your own refrigerator, before the storm.
  • You can live without HVAC for the length of a typical local outage.
  • You will add portable panels only if the event can last more than a day.
  • You want a 30-day-cookie brand with a US product page, not a no-name 500 Wh brick.

Who should skip these three

  • Automatic whole-home HVAC, sump, and furnace for days. That is a standby generator, not a carry-in station.
  • A 240 V well, dryer, or split-phase air handler. A 120 V explorer cannot feed it. The Anker 240 V fork is the F3800 (3,840 Wh, 6,000 W, 9,000 W surge, 120/240 V, 132.3 lb, $2,199.98). Also see best expandable home batteries.
  • You only camp twice a year and resent 40–62 lb. Rent, or buy a much smaller pack we do not feature.
  • You wanted a roof-tied wall pack. Tesla Powerwall and rooftop solar are out of scope on this desk.

What we skip on purpose

  • EcoFlow as a featured pick. The hardware can be competitive. The cookie is often cited as 7 days. This category is a weeks-long research loop. We prefer roughly 30-day programs (Jackery, BLUETTI, Anker SOLIX). EcoFlow is not a featured partner here.
  • No-name sub-$200 stations. Below the high-ticket constraint, and usually below the surge you need for a fridge.
  • Tesla Powerwall. Not portable. Not in-scope. We will not rank it against a wheeled case.
  • Other Jackery, BLUETTI, or Anker models. If it is not one of the named SKUs on this site, we do not pretend we reviewed it.

We have not personally cycled every SKU through a winter. Rankings are job-based plus documented electrical constraints: voltage, nameplate watts, nameplate watt-hours, weight, expansion, and dated street price. Test your house before the storm.

Honest caveats

2 kWh is a short evening if you add a space heater. Expandability helps days, not magic. Firmware, extra-pack stock, and sale prices move. Affiliate hops may earn a commission — disclosure. Corrections: contact.
