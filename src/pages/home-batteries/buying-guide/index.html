Buying guide · Home batteries

Write the circuit list before you fall in love with a kilowatt-hour number.

Home battery marketing leads with capacity. Houses fail on voltage (120 vs 240), surge, and recharge. Use this order.

  1. Separate 120 V and 240 V loads. If the well or dryer is non-negotiable, you need a split-phase inverter class (often Anker SOLIX F3800-class), not a 120 V explorer brick.
  2. Estimate watt-hours per outage. Fridge ~1–2 kWh/day, electronics much less, space heat disastrously more. Multiply by days you refuse to shed.
  3. Add starting watts. Same compressor lesson as power stations.
  4. Plan the house hook. Inlet box + interlock, or a listed transfer switch. Not a dryer-outlet cheat.
  5. Plan recharge. Wall AC if the grid flickers back; portable panels if it does not. Match Voc to the charge port.
  6. Prefer LiFePO4 for home-backup cycling unless you have a specific reason not to.

Featured carts: Anker SOLIX when 240 V and expansion matter, Bluetti when modular towers fit the garage, Jackery when you want a simpler expandable 120 V ecosystem. Read disclosure.

This is not a Powerwall buying guide. It is not a rooftop interconnection guide. If an installer is already quoting a wall pack with a roof, you are on the wrong site — by design.
