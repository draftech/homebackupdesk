Buying guide · Home batteries

Write the circuit list before you fall in love with a kilowatt-hour number.

Home battery marketing leads with capacity. Houses fail on voltage (120 vs 240), surge, and recharge.

  1. Separate 120 V and 240 V loads. If the well or dryer is non-negotiable, you need split-phase — on this desk, that is the Anker SOLIX F3800, not a 120 V explorer brick.
  2. Estimate watt-hours per outage. Fridge ~1–2 kWh/day, electronics much less, space heat disastrously more.
  3. Add starting watts. Same compressor lesson as power stations.
  4. Plan the house hook. Inlet box + interlock, or a listed transfer switch. Not a dryer-outlet cheat.
  5. Plan recharge. Wall AC if the grid flickers back; portable panels if it does not.
  6. Prefer LiFePO4 / LFP for home-backup cycling unless you have a specific reason not to.
