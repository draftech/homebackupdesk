Same class, three ecosystems

Shoppers land on a vs page hoping one brand is secretly better at chemistry. It is not. Jackery lists LiFePO4. BLUETTI lists LiFePO4. Anker lists LFP. That is the same lithium iron phosphate family. Cycle-life slogans on ads are not specs we copy. We copy nameplate watt-hours, inverter watts, weight, voltage, expansion, and the street price on the US page dated August 26, 2026.

Expansion maps

Watt-hours on day one are almost a tie: 2,042.8 Wh (Jackery), 2,048 Wh (BLUETTI), 2,048 Wh (Anker). The ecosystems diverge after the first pack.

  • Jackery. Expansion SKU we name is the Battery Pack 2000 Plus. We do not invent a kilowatt-hour ceiling Jackery did not print on the core page we copied. Plan on buying the extra pack as a real second product, not a slide in a brochure.
  • BLUETTI. The AC200L page lists expandability up to 8,192 Wh. That is the largest 120 V ceiling in this trio. Extra modules still feed a 2,400 W, 120 V inverter.
  • Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2. Expansion is up to 4 kWh. Half the BLUETTI ceiling, still double the base pack. Useful for a longer 120 V outage. Not a reason to skip split-phase if you need it.

Stock and firmware matter more than brand loyalty. An extra battery that is backordered is not expandability. Check the live cart, then check it again the week you buy.

Weight and moving the box

Weight is the spec people ignore in a browser and remember on a stair. Anker lists 41.7 lb. Jackery lists 61.5 lb. BLUETTI lists 62.4 lb. That is about 20 lb between the lightest and the two heavier cases.

None of these is a backpack. 41.7 lb is still a two-hand carry. 62 lb is a “park it and run a cord” machine for most households. If the station will live in a garage and feed the kitchen through a rated extension cord, buy on inverter and expansion, not on a scale. If you will lift it into a closet, a car, or a second-floor apartment, the Anker weight is the ecosystem argument, not a lifestyle caption.

Voltage: the 120 V trio vs the F3800 fork

All three featured stations are 120 V. That sentence is the whole well-pump conversation. Extra kilowatt-hours do not change the waveform from split-phase to wishful thinking. A 240 V well, dryer, or condenser is not a firmware update away.

Anker’s 240 V product on this site is not a fourth member of the 2 kWh trio. It is a fork. The Anker SOLIX F3800 is 3,840 Wh, 6,000 W continuous, 9,000 W surge, 120/240 V, 132.3 lb, street $2,199.98 on August 26, 2026. It belongs with home batteries and the expandable home battery list. Do not average it into a “best 2 kWh station” score. If you need automatic whole-home HVAC for days, leave batteries and read whole-house generators.

Panel families

Stay in-family for cables and Voc. Nameplate watts are lab numbers. Open-circuit voltage is what kills an input. Matching rules are in the portable solar panel buying guide. Named folders we carry:

  • Jackery SolarSaga 200W. 200 W, 14.33 lb, IP68, street $379 (was $429). Voc was not clearly listed on the page we copied, so we leave it blank. See the panel best-of.
  • BLUETTI SP200L. 200 W, Voc 24.6 V, 17.15 lb, street $399. IP was not clearly listed. Still check the AC200L solar window before you series a second panel.
  • Anker SOLIX PS400. 400 W, 35.3 lb, IP67, street $699.99. Voc blank on the page we copied. Twice the nameplate of the 200 W folders, and twice the carry. Confirm the C2000 Gen 2 solar input first.

Jackery: cart, cable, 3,000 W

Jackery’s ecosystem pitch is recognition plus a matched SolarSaga cable. The Explorer 2000 Plus is the core we name: 2,042.8 Wh, 3,000 W continuous, 61.5 lb, 120 V, LiFePO4, Battery Pack 2000 Plus, $899 with a $2,199 compare-at on August 26, 2026. Surge is blank on our desk because the live page was unclear or conflicting.

3,000 W continuous is the electrical reason to stay in this family if your simultaneous 120 V loads are the worry. It is not 240 V. It is not a pad generator. It is a heavier 2 kWh case with more inverter than the 2,400 W pair.

Choose Jackery when you want that inverter, the Battery Pack 2000 Plus path, and a SolarSaga 200W you will actually fold up before hail. Full review: Explorer 2000 Plus.

BLUETTI: stack 120 V kilowatt-hours

BLUETTI’s ecosystem pitch is modular watt-hours on a 120 V inverter. The AC200L is the core we name: 2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, 62.4 lb, expandable to 8,192 Wh, $899. Surge is blank. Same honesty rule as Jackery.

8,192 Wh is a lot of refrigerator-and-network time. It is zero well-pump time if the pump is 240 V. Read the extra-battery map on the live page the week you buy. We do not name other BLUETTI cores here.

Choose BLUETTI when you will spend on extra modules and an SP200L, and you have already accepted 120 V. Full review: AC200L.

Anker SOLIX: light 120 V core, separate 240 V cabinet

Anker’s ecosystem pitch is two products that should not be mashed into one score. The C2000 Gen 2 is the 120 V, 2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, 4,000 W peak, 41.7 lb, LFP, up to 4 kWh, $999.99 station. The F3800 is the 120/240 V cabinet in the home-battery silo. Pick one job.

If you want the lightest featured 2 kWh case and a printed peak watt, stay on the C2000 Gen 2. If the house hook is split-phase, leave this page and read the F3800. Do not buy the light station hoping it grows a second hot leg.

Choose Anker SOLIX when weight and a documented 4,000 W peak matter, or when you are ready to shop the F3800 instead. Full 120 V review: C2000 Gen 2.
