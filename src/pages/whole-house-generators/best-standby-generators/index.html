Quick picks by job

Most 1,500–2,500 sq ft homes that will shed HVAC

Air-cooled standby, roughly 9–12 kW, propane or natural gas, automatic transfer switch. Covers lights, refrigeration, well (if 240 V is in the load calc), and an air handler — not necessarily two outdoor condensers at once.

Large homes, heat pumps, or “run it like the grid”

Liquid-cooled, often 20–24 kW and up. Quieter at the pad, longer expected duty, much higher install. This is the class people mean when they say they do not want to manage circuits.

Who an air-cooled 10–12 kW is for

  • You will accept a smart load-shed module or you do not run two condensers during an outage.
  • You want automatic start when the grid dies, not a cord in the rain.
  • Your gas meter or propane tank can actually feed the BTU load.

Who should skip standby entirely

  • You mainly want a fridge, CPAP, and internet. That is a power station job.
  • You cannot place a unit 5+ feet from openings or your HOA will fight the pad.
  • You thought “home battery” meant Tesla Powerwall. Different category, out of scope.

Honest caveats

Nameplate kW assumes a clean power factor and a defined altitude/temperature. A 4-ton heat pump’s locked-rotor amps can still brown out a generator that “looks big enough.” Whole-house does not mean unlimited. Exercise the unit monthly. Budget oil, filters, and a service contract. Featured affiliates (Jackery, Bluetti, Anker SOLIX) are the wrong cart for this silo.
