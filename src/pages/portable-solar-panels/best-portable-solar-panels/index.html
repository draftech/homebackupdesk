Best-of · Portable panels

The best portable panel is the one your station will accept at noon without over-voltage.

Nameplate watts are lab numbers. Buyers lose money on open-circuit voltage, extra connectors, and a 35.3 lb folder they will not carry to the driveway. Stay in-family with the station. Check Voc.

This is a best-of for folding portable panels that refill Jackery, BLUETTI, and Anker SOLIX stations. It is not a roof ranking. It is not a briefcase ranking — we do not have a named briefcase SKU with an official photo, so that format lives on folding vs briefcase as a format discussion, not a fake third trophy.

Matching beats mixing. A SolarSaga cable into a Jackery input is the boring, correct default if you already own an Explorer. An SP200L into a BLUETTI input is the same idea. A PS400 into an Anker SOLIX input is the same idea at twice the nameplate and more than twice the carry of a 200 W folder. Cross-brand MC4 adapters exist. They do not excuse skipping the station’s Voc ceiling.

  1. Read the station’s solar window — max watts, max Voc, sometimes max Isc. Cold sun raises Voc. Leave margin.
  2. Prefer the same-brand panel so the cable and the marketing voltage range are meant for each other.
  3. Size watts to the outage, not to a “400 W sounds twice as good” reflex. Overnight fridge backup is a battery problem.
  4. Weigh the packed kit. 14.33 lb and 17.15 lb are folders most adults can carry. 35.3 lb is a two-hand project.
  5. Plan theft and weather on the same day you plan watt-hours.

Voc warning. Open-circuit voltage is the spec that fries charge controllers. Series wiring adds Voc. Parallel wiring adds current. If the live station page lists a maximum Voc of, say, a number you can actually read, your panel string must stay under it in the cold. Because Jackery and Anker pages were unclear on Voc for the SolarSaga 200W and the PS400, we show an em dash rather than a guessed volt. Confirm on the live product page before you buy jumpers. SP200L’s 24.6 V is listed — still check before you series two of them.

Best 200 W Jackery match: SolarSaga 200W

Jackery Explorer owners should start here. 200 W nameplate, 14.33 lb, IP68, $379 against a $429 compare-at on August 26, 2026. The job is a matched cable into a Jackery station: unfold, aim, fold before hail. Voc blank in our table. Do not copy a Voc from a reseller listing and treat it as gospel. Review: SolarSaga 200W. Station: Explorer 2000 Plus. See SolarSaga price.

Best 200 W BLUETTI match: SP200L

BLUETTI AC200L owners should start here. 200 W nameplate, 24.6 V Voc, 17.15 lb, IP blank because the manufacturer page was unclear, $399. MC4 into a BLUETTI input is the intended path. Heavier than the SolarSaga by a few pounds; still a one-person folder. Check Voc against the station before you series-wire. Review: SP200L. Station: AC200L. See BLUETTI panel price.

Best 400 W Anker match: PS400 — if you will carry 35.3 lb

Anker SOLIX owners who need more nameplate than a 200 W folio, and who will actually haul 35.3 lb to the driveway, look at the PS400. 400 W, IP67, $699.99, Voc blank. Twice the sticker watts of the 200 W class is not twice the daily kilowatt-hours in winter haze, and it is not free if the station’s solar watt ceiling is lower than the blanket. Confirm the window on the C2000 Gen 2 or the F3800 before you assume 400 W in. Review: PS400. See PS400 price.

Who a 200 W folding panel is for

  • You will be home to tilt it a couple of times a day.
  • Your station’s max solar input is at least that wattage at a legal Voc.
  • Weight around 14–17 lb is acceptable.
  • You want the Jackery or BLUETTI cable story, not a mystery MC4 kit from a marketplace tab.

Who should skip portable panels

  • Outages are four hours at night. A panel does nothing. Buy Wh instead.
  • You wanted roof production. Out of scope.
  • You will leave four figures of glass on a street-facing lawn unsupervised.
  • You will not carry 35.3 lb, but you were about to click the 400 W card anyway.

What we skip on purpose

  • EcoFlow panels as featured picks. Hardware can be fine. Cookie window is a 7-day risk for this research cycle.
  • No-name 400 W blankets. Below the high-ticket constraint we want, and often over-Voc for the stations we name.
  • Rooftop modules, optimizers, microinverters. Out of scope.
  • A named briefcase SKU. No official photo in the catalog, so no fake card.

Yield, matching, and the driveway

Yield is weather. A 200 W panel in decent sun might return on the order of 0.6–1.2 kWh in a day, not 200 × 8. The PS400’s 400 W nameplate does not mint 3 kWh on a gray driveway. Partial shade on one cell can crater a string. Kickstands fall over in gusts. Folding fabric is the format that makes watts; it is also the format that sails.

Match the panel to the station you already chose, then match the job to daylight. The Explorer 2000 Plus (2,042.8 Wh, 3,000 W, 61.5 lb, Battery Pack 2000 Plus) is a Jackery tank; the SolarSaga is the refill. The AC200L (2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, up to 8,192 Wh) is a BLUETTI tank; the SP200L is the refill. The C2000 Gen 2 and the F3800 are Anker tanks; the PS400 is the refill if 35.3 lb is acceptable and the solar window holds 400 W. Cross-brand MC4 jumpers do not make a Voc problem go away.

A four-hour evening outage is still a battery purchase. Panels earn their keep on multi-day events with midday sun. If your outages are ice storms with no sky, you need watt-hours or fuel, not another folio. If your lot is a theft magnet, you need a plan to bring the kit in, not a 400 W sail facing the street.

Series versus parallel belongs in the buying guide, but the best-of rule is short: one matched panel first. Two panels are a wiring project. Series adds Voc — the spec we left blank on SolarSaga and PS400 rather than guess. Parallel adds current. If you cannot say which one you are doing, you are not ready for a second blanket. Read the buying guide before you click a Y-cable.

IP ratings are not hail insurance. Jackery lists IP68 on the SolarSaga. Anker lists IP67 on the PS400. BLUETTI’s SP200L IP cell is blank because the page was unclear. Fold before the storm you can see. Do not treat a wet driveway photo as a product shot; the named SKUs above are the official photographs.
