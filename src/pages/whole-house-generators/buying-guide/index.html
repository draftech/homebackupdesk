Buying guide · Generators

Size the loads, then the gas line, then the brochure.

The buying mistake on whole-house generators is reversing that order. Dealers will happily quote a 24 kW unit for a 9 kW problem — or a 10 kW unit for a heat pump that will never start.

  1. List running watts and starting watts. Air handlers, well pumps, and condensers dominate. Lights do not.
  2. Decide what you will shed. “Whole house” with a lockout kit is still a valid, cheaper design.
  3. Confirm fuel energy. A generator that needs more BTU than the meter or tank can deliver will sag under load.
  4. Walk the pad. Distances to windows, property lines, and the meter are code items, not vibes.
  5. Price install before equipment. See cost.
  6. Plan exercise and oil. A unit that sits for 11 months is a project, not insurance.

Fuel

Natural gas is set-and-forget if the utility stays up (it usually does in storms). Propane is independent of the gas utility but you own the tank level. Gasoline belongs on portables, not on a pad plant you expect to start itself.

Electrical

An automatic transfer switch is the product. The engine is the accessory. Service-entrance ATS vs load-shed ATS vs a managed panel changes both cost and what actually stays on. This is licensed electrician work in almost every US jurisdiction.
