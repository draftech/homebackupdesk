What folding means here

A folding portable panel is a folio of cells with fabric or polymer hinges, a handle, and usually kickstands. You unzip or unclip, unfold two or four leaves, prop the array, and aim. Packed, it is a flat-ish suitcase that slides into a trunk. Unpacked, it is a sail. That sail is why 200–400 W is practical on a driveway and miserable in a crosswind.

Jackery’s SolarSaga 200W is the lightest named folder we list: 14.33 lb, IP68, $379 (was $429) on August 26, 2026. BLUETTI’s SP200L is 17.15 lb, 24.6 V Voc, $399, IP blank because the page was unclear. Anker’s PS400 is the large folio: 400 W nameplate, 35.3 lb, IP67, $699.99. Voc is blank on SolarSaga and PS400 because manufacturer pages conflicted or were unclear. Those three are the folding aisle. They are not briefcases.

What a briefcase is, without inventing a SKU

A hard briefcase solar kit is typically two rigid leaves that open like a laptop and sit as a triangle. The case is the kickstand. Packed shape is a thick rectangle, often with latches, sometimes with a handle that actually belongs on luggage. Nameplate watts in this format are commonly lower than a four-leaf folio — think a smaller array that survives being leaned against a picnic table.

We are not going to assign a brand, a watt number, or a price to “the briefcase” as if we had a featured SKU. Without an official photo we will not put a card in the grid. If a manufacturer later gives us a clean studio shot and a dated spec sheet that fits the catalog rules, it can join the list. Until then, treat briefcase as a shape you might still buy after you do your own Voc homework — not as a product we ranked.

Wind, setup, and giving up

The failure mode that does not show up on a wattage sticker is you. A folio that needs ten minutes of fidgeting will get left in the garage after the second gust. A briefcase that aims itself as soon as the latches open will still be out there at 2 p.m. That is not a moral judgment. It is why format is the vs that changes a day of charging.

Home backup on a driveway is usually the folding job. You are trying to put meaningful watt-hours into a ~2 kWh station before sunset, or into a larger home kit in daylight. The SolarSaga and SP200L are sized for that if the sun cooperates. The PS400 is the same idea with more glass and 35.3 lb of reminder that “portable” is a marketing word. A hard briefcase is the format you consider when the site is camp, beach wind, or a kid-and-dog yard that will destroy hinges — and when you accept that you may put fewer watt-hours back.

  1. Decide whether the goal is kilowatt-hours or survivability. Folding wins the first. Briefcases often win the second.
  2. Walk the actual ground. Flat driveway with a south shot favors a folio. Gusty deck favors a low triangle.
  3. Check Voc against the station regardless of format. Series jumpers are format-agnostic ways to kill an input.
  4. Weigh the packed kit the way you will carry it, not the way a studio lists it.

Voc on SolarSaga 200W and PS400 is blank in our catalog because manufacturer pages conflicted or were unclear. SP200L lists 24.6 V. Format does not waive that homework. See the buying guide.

Buy folding when

  • You are trying to put meaningful Wh back into a 2 kWh-class station before sunset.
  • You have a driveway or south wall you can babysit.
  • You want a named Jackery, BLUETTI, or Anker SOLIX folio with an official photo.

Consider a briefcase when

  • Wind or camping abuse would destroy a floppy folio.
  • You accept slower recharge in exchange for a lid that aims itself.
  • You are willing to shop without a featured card from this site.
  • Do not treat this page as a ranking of three briefcases. There are zero named briefcases here.
  • Do not buy a folio because it “looks like 400 W” if you will not carry 35.3 lb.
  • Do not leave either format as unsupervised lawn art.
  • Do not use a window as a charge plan.
