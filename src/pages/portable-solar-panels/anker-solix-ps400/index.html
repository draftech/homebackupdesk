Who the PS400 is for

  • You are in the Anker SOLIX aisle — C2000 Gen 2 or the F3800 — and you will confirm the solar watt and Voc window first.
  • You will carry 35.3 lb over a driveway, set kickstands, and rotate the folio. Not once in a product video. Repeatedly.
  • A 200 W folder is not enough nameplate for the daylight hours you actually get, and you accept that 400 W still under-delivers in haze.
  • You want IP67 quoted from Anker, not a no-name blanket.

Who should skip it

  • 35.3 lb is a non-starter. Buy a 200 W folder or buy watt-hours. Do not click 400 W as a trophy.
  • Your station’s solar input ceiling is well under 400 W. Extra glass is ballast.
  • Your station is Jackery or BLUETTI and you wanted the in-family 200 W cable — SolarSaga or SP200L.
  • Outages are short and after sunset. A panel does nothing then.

400 W versus real sun — and 35.3 lb

Nameplate 400 W is twice the SolarSaga and twice the SP200L on paper. Daily kilowatt-hours are not automatically twice, because sun angle, temperature, and how long you keep the array aimed are the real multipliers. In decent sun you might see a more useful midday punch than a 200 W folio. In winter haze you might see a disappointing trickle and still have carried 35.3 lb to get it.

Say the weight again: 35.3 lb. That is a car battery, a toddler, a bag of salt. It is why this review exists as its own page instead of a footnote on the 200 W class. Two-hand carry. Stairs are rude. Kickstands must hold a larger sail. When the folio wants to become a kite, 35.3 lb is both ballast and a hazard to shins.

IP67 is Anker’s listed rating. Water and dust, not hail, not theft. Street $699.99 on August 26, 2026. See PS400 price. Same price to you. Disclosure.

  1. Read the Anker station’s solar window — watts and Voc — on the live page for the C2000 Gen 2 or F3800 you actually own.
  2. Lift 35.3 lb in your head along the real route from closet to driveway. If that fails, stop.
  3. Do not invent Voc to justify series jumpers. Our cell is blank on purpose.
  4. Aim, babysit, fold. A 400 W sail is more panel to pick up when the gust wins.

Voc we will not guess

We left Voc blank for the PS400 because manufacturer pages conflicted or were unclear. The same rule applied to the SolarSaga 200W. The SP200L is the folder where we could print 24.6 V. Blank is not “probably 40 V.” Blank is “go read the live page and if you still cannot tell, do not series-wire.” Cold sun raises Voc. Charge-controller maxima are not suggestions.

Watt ceiling is the other half. If the station clips at a number below 400 W, you paid for glass it will not use. Confirm before you chase nameplate. Buying-guide version of this lecture: portable solar panel buying guide.

Job line from our catalog: twice the nameplate of the 200 W folders. Confirm the station’s solar window first. Weight 35.3 lb is the filter that belongs in every sentence about this SKU.

Pairing with Anker SOLIX stations

In-family is the point. The C2000 Gen 2 is the lighter ~2 kWh 120 V station (2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, 4,000 W peak, 41.7 lb). The F3800 is the split-phase home kit (3,840 Wh, 6,000 W, 9,000 W surge, 132.3 lb). Both can, in principle, take portable solar. Neither will take a Voc you guessed. Neither turns the PS400 into a roof.

A 3,840 Wh F3800 still needs a dull daily yield estimate. One 400 W folio in poor sun will not refill it by dinner. Extra panels mean extra 35.3 lb carries and extra Voc math. If the honest need is multi-day winter runtime without sun, you are back to battery vs standby, not a bigger blanket.
