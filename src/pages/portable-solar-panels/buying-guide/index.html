Buying guide · Portable panels

If Voc is over the station’s max, the panel is not a deal. It is a paperweight.

This is the silo where people fry charge controllers or wonder why a “400 W” blanket delivers 90 W. Buy in this order.

  1. Read the station’s solar input window. Max watts, max Voc, sometimes max Isc. Cold sun raises Voc — leave margin.
  2. Prefer the same-brand cable. SolarSaga into Jackery, SP200L into BLUETTI, PS400 into SOLIX. Hops: Jackery, BLUETTI, Anker SOLIX.
  3. Size watts to the job. Overnight fridge backup is a battery problem. Multi-day grid-down is a panel-plus-battery problem. A 200 W panel in decent sun might return on the order of 0.6–1.2 kWh/day, not 200 × 8.
  4. Count real portable weight. The PS400 is 35.3 lb on Anker’s page. Two 200 W folders plus stands is a project.
  5. Plan theft and weather. Fold it before hail. Do not leave it as lawn art.

Series vs parallel: series raises voltage (Voc risk), parallel raises current (connector and wire risk). If you cannot say which one you are doing, buy a single matched panel first.
