Buying guide · Power stations

Buy surge headroom first, watt-hours second, brand third.

Stations are simple products sold with complicated numbers. This is the order of operations.

  1. Running watts. Add the loads that will be on together. A fridge might be 150–250 W once spinning; a microwave is a short 1,000 W punch.
  2. Starting watts. Compressors can demand several times running watts for a second. If the inverter cannot do that second, the fridge never starts.
  3. Watt-hours. Rough runtime hours ≈ usable Wh ÷ running watts. LiFePO4 often lets you plan closer to 80–90% than old NMC packing.
  4. Recharge. Wall watts decide recovery between flickers. Solar is weather. Voc of the panel must stay under the station’s solar maximum.
  5. Expandability. Extra batteries beat guessing a 6 kWh outage on day one — if the extra pack is real and in stock.
  6. Ports. UPS pass-through, 12 V for CPAPs, and whether you need a 30 A TT-30 are lifestyle details, not afterthoughts.
