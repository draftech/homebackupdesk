Vs · Generators

Standby vs portable is really automatic vs you-in-the-driveway.

A standby generator sits on a pad, ties to a transfer switch, and starts when voltage dies. A portable generator makes comparable watts for less money and then asks you to do everything else correctly, including carbon monoxide.

What changes when you leave the pad and pick up a handle
Standby (automatic)Portable (manual)
StartATS senses outage, typically 10–30 secondsYou roll it out, start it, plug the inlet
FuelNatural gas or propane (no jerry cans)Gasoline and/or propane; gasoline goes stale
CircuitsWhole panel or a managed whole-home loadInlet box + interlock, or extension cords
CO riskOutdoor pad, still needs clearancesHigh if used in a garage or too close to windows
All-in costOften $7k–$20k+ installedOften $800–$4k plus a legal inlet
MaintenanceExercise, oil, dealer serviceSame oil reality, plus storage and carburetor issues

Choose standby when

  • Nobody in the house can (or should) start a machine at 2 a.m.
  • You have a well, sump, or medical load that cannot wait.
  • You already know install is in the budget — see cost.

Choose portable when

  • Outages are rare and you will actually maintain the unit.
  • You will pay an electrician for an inlet + interlock, not a death-cord in a window.
  • You might instead want a quiet LiFePO4 station for indoor-adjacent essentials.

Portable gas generators and portable power stations are not the same product. Stations have no exhaust and limited watt-hours. Generators have exhaust and a fuel tank. Do not run a portable generator in a garage “just for a minute.” If your real comparison is battery vs gas plant, use home battery vs standby.
